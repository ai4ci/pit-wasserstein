DeGroot & Fienberg (1983), *The Statistician* 32(1–2):12–22.

Origin of the **calibration and refinement** decomposition of forecast quality:
a forecaster is calibrated if outcomes occur at the stated rate, and among
calibrated forecasters the more refined (more concentrated) one is preferred.
They relate refinement to sufficiency in the comparison of experiments, and
connect both to proper scoring rules.

This is the ancestor of the "maximise sharpness subject to calibration"
paradigm that Gneiting, Balabdaoui & Raftery (2007) state for continuous
outcomes, which is the framing the paper uses for why a calibration-only
metric is worth having alongside a proper score.

**Why cite:** one-line intro citation for the calibration/refinement (later
calibration/sharpness) distinction — calibration is a necessary but not
sufficient condition, so $D^*_{pit}$ complements rather than replaces CRPS.
